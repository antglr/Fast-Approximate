Fast-Approximate BCM Study

With the BCM switched off, the charge of each shot can be inferred from that shot's own laser and screen measurements to 1–2.5% at 5–10 nC and about 13 pC at 0.1 nC. What is left is the BCM's own noise and a slow calibration drift. Forecasting from the past recovers only the trend.

What was done

The work went in four stages. Each stage answered a question that decided the next one.

Stage 1 · 00–04

Understand the data

HDF5 chunks of 100 shots: scalars (BCM, laser power meters, magnets, temperatures, humidity), RF waveforms, laser scope trace (2002 samples at 25 ps, a ~50-pulse train at ~1.5 GHz), BTV0125 screen images (1216 × 1936, 12-bit).

Stage 2 · 05–07

Correlate and align

Which instruments follow the charge, shot to shot and over time, and in which row each one is stored. Per-day feature selection (|r| ≥ 0.25 with BCM, ≤ 0.5 between features).

Stage 3 · 08–08_06

Virtual BCM

Infer y(t) from the same shot's x(t), as if the BCM were lost after 70% of each segment. Trend + shot-to-shot two-stage models, losses, physics constraints, ensembles.

Stage 4 · 09–10_01

Forecast from the past

How much of x(t) is in x(t−N) (t-SNE, autocorrelation), then forecast y(t) from data up to t−N, against an oracle-trend floor.

What the data showed

  • The laser drives the charge. Shot to shot, the lab laser power meter follows BCM_GUN at r = 0.83 and the screen intensity at r = 0.79 (run 07-46-07).
  • Instruments are not stored in the same row. BCM and screen are aligned; the laser scope, the power meters and the RF waveforms are stored one row late. Every model shifts them back by one row.
  • The one-row shift is not exact for every shot. In segments 1, 5 and 7 the laser one row earlier still correlates with the BCM almost as strongly (0.37 vs 0.47, 0.38 vs 0.39, 0.67 vs 0.63).
  • The laser scope repeats itself. 23% of its traces are copies of the previous shot. They are flagged and dropped.
  • The screen needed care. Clusters of hot pixels faked peaks in 27 shots, and the faint halo makes the RMS size depend on the cut (1.7 mm with no cut, 0.28 mm at 5% of the peak).
  • Klystron, magnets and environment do not explain the charge shot to shot. Temperature and humidity only follow it over tens of minutes.
  • Each run has its own operating point. On a t-SNE map the runs form separate islands, so a model has to be fitted per segment.

What made the difference

  • Splitting trend and shot-to-shot. Each stage gets its own target and its own model.
  • The concordance loss (CCC) for the shot-to-shot stage. MSE shrinks the predicted fluctuations to 55–65% of their real size; CCC brings them to about 90%.
  • Training the trend stage on recent shots only. This took segment 1 from 196 to 121 pC by removing a +0.5 nC overshoot.
  • The physics constraint. Without it the model predicted less charge for more laser power on 40–98% of shots. With it this never happens, and the segment where the model has to extrapolate improves (230 → 214 pC).

What did not help

  • A second model trained to predict the difference (residual correction): worse, even on out-of-fold differences.
  • One model pooled over all segments: much worse. The runs behave differently.
  • Max-error loss: worst on almost every metric, max error included.
  • Longer memory for the trend (rolling means, TCN), wider networks, GRU over past shots: mixed at best.
  • Tuned ensembles: chosen on validation, neutral on test.

Most of the inference comes from the screen intensity (BTV0125). A BTV screen intercepts the beam, so check whether the screen can stay in during operation before relying on this model. A laser-and-RF-only version has not been tested yet.

Forecasting from the past

Everything is known up to shot t−N; the task is to forecast the charge of shot t. The past carries the slow drift of the state (laser power keeps r ≈ 0.5 out to 50 shots) but almost none of its shot-to-shot part (r ≈ 0 after 1–2 shots). So the best possible forecast knows the trend exactly. Its error, the oracle-trend floor, is the BCM's own shot-to-shot spread.

Two-step model x(t−N) → x̂(t) → y(t)

Step 2 works: given the true x(t) it matches the virtual BCM. Step 1 is the limit: x̂(t) recovers at best R² ≈ 0.35 (laser power), and about 0 for the spot shape and RF. The forecast is no better than the 51-shot BCM mean, at any model size.

Where forecasting stands

Up to N ≈ 20 the forecasts sit within 1–7% of the floor in every segment. At N = 50–100 the remaining error is trend drift that the past does not announce (about 170 pC of slow error in segments 1 and 5 at N = 100). The features add little beyond the BCM history and overfit in segment 3.

Recommendations and open points

  • Freeze the virtual BCM at C with the physics constraint. Model choice no longer changes the result beyond the noise of a single test.
  • Re-extract the datasets with per-shot timestamp alignment (scripts/pulses.py) on the cluster. The leftover laser timing jitter sits exactly in the segments with the largest errors (1, 5, 7).
  • Make the evaluation robust: several cut times per segment, and train on earlier runs, test on a later one.
  • Add an uncertainty band to each inferred shot and a flag when the inputs leave the training range.
  • Test a version without the screen (laser and RF only).
  • For forecasting, use the simplest model near the floor: an EWMA or the BCM-history MLP, plus a band that widens with N.

Limits of these numbers: one 70% cut per segment, so differences of a few percent are within test noise. The data in data/ was replaced once during the study (run 11-48-33 → 07-46-07); everything here uses the later data and the team's stitched segments.

Data preparation

Segments
Runs from 2026-10-07 to 10-09, cut into contiguous stretches of clean chunks (team's CLEAN_CUTS.toml). A new segment starts at every new run and wherever chunks were removed. 8 segments, 63,327 shots.
Timing alignment
Lag scans of every quantity against BCM_GUN showed two groups. BCM and screen are stored in the same row; the laser scope, laser power meters and RF waveforms peak one row later. That group is shifted back by one row inside each segment, so each row is one shot.
Valid shots
A shot is dropped when the laser scope repeated its previous trace (23% of shots), when the camera repeated its image, or when it is the last row of a segment (no partner after the shift).
Beam shots
Shots below 20% of the training median charge (no beam or a BCM glitch, down to −3.8 nC) are left out of training and of the scores. They would otherwise dominate the max error and break log-scale models.

Features (16 per shot)

Laser energy
lab_laser_power from the power meter. The laser scope gives a second estimate: subtract the baseline (mean before 5 ns), then project the trace on the mean pulse train of the segment (5.6–39.8 ns, about 50 pulses at ~1.5 GHz). This matched filter averages over all pulses and beats a plain integral (shot-to-shot r 0.44 vs 0.33 with the charge).
RF
PGLA_drop = PGLA sample 631 − sample 636: the jump on the crest, at two fixed indices taken from the mean waveform. PKI15A = mean of the klystron forward amplitude over its flat top (samples 300–640).
Screen (BTV0125)
Crop to a region of interest, subtract the background (median of the top 150 rows), apply a 3×3 median filter against hot pixels. The amplitude is the peak of a lightly smoothed copy (Gaussian σ = 3 px). The beam mask is the largest connected blob above 5% of that peak, which removes hot-pixel clusters. Intensity, centroid and RMS sizes are moments inside the mask, in mm (0.0107 mm/pixel).
Spot shape
Eigen-decomposition of the intensity-weighted 2×2 covariance inside the same mask: RMS size along the spot's own axes (sigma_major, sigma_minor), tilt of the major axis, ellipticity = minor / major.
Image SVD
SVD_s1…s6: the 6 largest singular values of the 4×4-binned image. They are computed per image with no fitting, so they cannot leak information from the test shots.

Testing and scores

Every model is tested the same way, as if the BCM stopped working at a given time.

one segment, in time order: [ fit: 0 → 56% ][ validation: 56 → 70% ] | cut at 70% | [ test: 70 → 100% ] train models choose and stop BCM "lost" score once
Chronological split
Models are trained before the cut and scored once on the shots after it. Nothing after the cut is used for training. All choices (training window, model, ensemble weights, early stopping) are made on the validation block, never on the test shots.
Error
Std of inferred − measured charge on the test shots, in pC. Bias is its mean.
Slow and fast parts
The difference is split into its 51-shot rolling mean (slow: trend or calibration error) and the rest (fast: shot-to-shot error).
Shot-to-shot fluctuation
A quantity minus its centred 51-shot rolling mean. The fluctuation score compares inferred and measured fluctuations: correlation, size ratio (std inferred / std measured, 1 = right size), and explained variance.
Concordance (CCC)
Rewards following each swing and reproducing its size at the same time:
CCC = 2·cov(p, y) / ( var p + var y + (mean p − mean y)² ) 1 = perfect, 0 = no agreement

BCM noise floor (three-cornered hat)

The BCM reading is the true charge plus BCM noise. The screen intensity and the laser energy see the same charge with independent noise. From the three shot-to-shot fluctuations, the variance of the common true charge in the BCM reading is:

var(true charge in BCM) = cov(BCM, screen) · cov(BCM, laser) / cov(screen, laser) noise floor [pC] = √(1 − var(true charge in BCM) / var BCM) · std(BCM fluctuation)

A perfect model would still differ from the BCM by this amount, so the fast part of any error cannot go much below it. It assumes linear relations and independent noise, so it is an estimate: in two segments the model ends slightly below it.

Virtual BCM model (final model C)

y(t) ≈ trend(t) + fluctuation(t) trend(t) = mean of stage-1 predictions over the previous 51 shots fluctuation(t) = stage 2( x(t) − mean of x over the previous 51 shots, x(t) )
Stage 1, trend
Ridge regression on the standardised features, then gradient-boosted trees on what Ridge misses. Trained only on the last W shots before the end of the training data, with W ∈ {all, 4000, 2000, 1000} chosen by the trend error on validation. Recent shots calibrate better because the instrument-to-BCM relation drifts.
Stage 2, fluctuation
Target: y(t) − mean of y over the previous 51 shots. Inputs: the same deviation of each feature plus the raw features, standardised on the fit shots. Model: MLP with 2 hidden layers of 64 units (GELU, dropout 0.1), AdamW, early stopping on validation, trained on the CCC loss. The average of 6 seeds, with a bias correction on the fit shots.
Physics constraint
More laser power must give more charge. Stage 1: the Ridge coefficient of laser power is constrained to ≥ 0 (bounded least squares), and the trees get a monotonic constraint on it. Stage 2: a training penalty on any negative slope, mean(relu(−∂output / ∂laser input)).
Tried and not kept
Log-scale targets, Huber regression, extra-trees, kNN, kernel ridge, GRU over 8 past shots, TCN over 128 shots, rolling-mean features, MSE / std / max-error losses, a correction model on out-of-fold differences, pooling and pretraining across segments, tuned ensembles.

Forecasting from the past

Setting
BCM and instruments known up to shot t−N; forecast y(t) for N = 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100.
Oracle-trend floor
Std of y − 51-shot centred rolling mean of y on the test shots: the error of a forecast that knows the trend exactly but nothing about the shot-to-shot part.
History features
At the origin t−N: the last 8 BCM readings, BCM exponential moving averages (EWMA) at α = 1/2, 1/8, 1/32, 1/128, 1/512 (time scales of about 2 to 512 shots), the last 4 feature vectors, and the feature EWMAs at the same scales.
Models
EWMA of the BCM with α chosen per N on validation; gradient boosting with N as an input; multi-horizon MLP (123k parameters, 3 hidden layers, N as an input so one model learns all horizons, target = residual against the BCM EWMA at α = 1/32), with and without features, and pretrained on all segments then fine-tuned; tuned ensemble with weights ≥ 0 summing to 1 per segment and horizon (SLSQP on the validation error).
Two-step model
Encoder over the last 32 feature vectors up to t−N → x̂(t) (16 features, with its own loss) → second network on x̂(t), y(t−N) and the 51-shot BCM mean → y(t). 207k parameters, trained end to end.

Exploration tools

Lag scans
Shot-to-shot correlation of X(t + L) with BCM(t) for L = −5 … +5, after removing a 51-shot rolling mean. A peak at L = +1 means X is stored one row late. A shift is applied only above 5σ, because picking the best of 11 lags lets noise through at 3σ.
Image PCA
SVD of the 4×4-binned, background-subtracted images of a segment. The first components are beam position on the screen; brightness and spot size come next.
t-SNE
2-D map of the 16 standardised features (perplexity 40, PCA initialisation), coloured by segment, time and charge. Used to see run-level clusters and whether consecutive shots stay close.
Neighbour test
How often x(t−N) is among the 10 nearest neighbours of x(t) in the 16-D space, compared with chance (10 / block size).
